The health ministry’s first 180 days, in its own words. One word missing: measles.

The government’s six-month health scorecard lists repairs, recruitment steps and reform plans. It leaves out a measles outbreak that filled hospital wards and pushed families into debt, heartbreak and despair.

This report follows the distance between those promises and the care people received: an outbreak that kept sending children to hospital, families paying for treatment, and a health system short of the staff and services they needed.

The scorecard ended. The outbreak did not.

For a family beside a hospital bed, the test of a health system is immediate: can their child get care? By the time the government closed its 180-day scorecard, reported measles and measles-like deaths had reached 908. By 17 October, the toll was 1,189. Behind that rising count were children needing treatment and families waiting with them.

A campaign is only as strong as its reach

On paper, the measles-rubella drive beat its target. But the Vitamin A campaign that followed, for children of the same age group, reached 38.83 lakh more children than the measles jab did.
